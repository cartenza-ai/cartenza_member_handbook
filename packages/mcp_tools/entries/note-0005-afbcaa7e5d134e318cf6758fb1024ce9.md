## capella-fabric: Seeing a Diagram, Not Just Reading About It

Everything else in this package turns a model into **text** — elements, relationships, YAML. `render_diagram_view` does something different: it renders a diagram from the model and gives you back a **link to a page you can open**, with the picture on it and a table of what's in it.

That matters most for **document generation**. A diagram your agent can describe is useful in conversation; a diagram you can put in a document is useful to everyone downstream.

### How it works

```
clone_capella_repo → search_model_objects (find the diagram) → render_diagram_view → open the link
```

Pass the diagram's ID. What comes back is small — a handful of fields, including:

- **`url`** — the page. Open it in a browser, or paste it to a colleague.
- **`layout`** — `stored` or `computed`. See below.
- **`elements`** — how many things the picture shows.

**Your agent should hand you the link, not the picture.** A rendered diagram is tens of kilobytes of encoded image; relayed through a conversation it costs a great deal and renders no better than the page does. If your agent starts describing the image to you instead of giving you a URL, see "If you don't get a link" below.

### The link is temporary, and that's deliberate

It carries its own signature and **expires in about four hours**, along with the session that made it. It is not a permanent home for a diagram and shouldn't be pasted into a document that outlives the afternoon. Anyone with the link can open the page for as long as it lives — so treat it the way you'd treat any link to a model view: fine for a colleague on the project, not for a public channel.

If you need the diagram to **last**, have your agent record it as an artifact rather than keeping the link.

### Two kinds of diagram, and why the picture differs

Capella models contain two quite different things that both get called diagrams.

**Diagrams someone drew** — the ones in the model tree, laid out by hand in Capella. These render **from the positions saved in the model**, so the picture matches what the author arranged: same boxes, same places. The page says so in its footer, and `layout` reads `stored`.

One honest limitation of drawing from stored positions: **a connector may not meet its box exactly.** A line can stop a pixel or two short of the edge it points at. The shapes and the arrangement are the author's; the very last pixel of a line sometimes isn't. If that ever looks wrong enough to matter in a document, say so — it's a known trade, made on purpose, and there is a better answer available if the need is real.

**Views generated on request** — context diagrams and their relatives, which don't exist in the model until something asks for one. These are laid out automatically when you ask, and `layout` reads `computed`. A first render can take half a minute while the layout engine works; later ones are quicker.

Both are real views of the same model. Neither is more correct than the other — they answer different questions. Ask for a drawn diagram when you want *what the team drew*; ask for a generated view when you want *what connects to this element right now*.

### If you don't get a link

**Your client froze its list of tools when it connected.** Every MCP client does this — Claude Desktop, the ChatGPT connector, and command-line agents alike. A tool added since you connected is invisible, and so is a **new field on a tool you already have**, which is the harder case to spot: the tool works, it just quietly lacks the thing you're reading about.

If `render_diagram_view` isn't there, or it runs but returns no `url`, **reconnect the connector** — remove and re-add it, or restart the client. That is the whole fix. It is not a sign that anything is broken on the Cartenza side, and no amount of rephrasing the request will conjure a field your client doesn't know exists.

### Keep the record separate

As everywhere else in this package: the **diagram** comes from capella-fabric; the **reason you went looking for it** — what you noticed in it, what you decided — belongs in `knowledge-repo`. A link that expires in four hours is a poor record of a decision that doesn't.

**Reference:** "capella-fabric: Reading and Changing a Model Safely" in this package, for finding a diagram in the first place and for the `layer`-versus-`viewpoint` trap when searching for one. capella-fabric's own `instructions` field carries the current argument list.
